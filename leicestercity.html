Leicester City Analysis

Tactical Insight

Leicester City had the lowest passing accuracy (70%) of any champion in history. They didn’t care about keeping the ball; they cared about moving it forward.

Key Player: N’Golo Kanté

Kanté made 156 interceptions this season—more than any other player in Europe’s top 5 leagues. He was effectively a two-man midfield on his own.

This animation illustrates the “Trap and Spring” tactic that defined Leicester City’s title-winning season. By allowing the opponent (Red) to push their defensive line high up the pitch, Leicester baited them into leaving vast amounts of empty space behind their defenders. The “Trigger” occurs when Drinkwater bypasses the entire opposing midfield with a single pass into that open area—not to a player’s feet, but into space for Jamie Vardy to chase. Vardy’s superior speed allows him to win the footrace against the backpedaling defense, turning a defensive situation into a 1-on-1 goal in seconds, perfectly demonstrating how Leicester prioritized vertical efficiency over possession.

Verticality in Action

Leicester didn’t build slowly; they struck instantly. This goal vs Liverpool perfectly illustrates the “One Pass” philosophy.

This animation visualizes the “Macro” story of the season: consistency. While the tactical animation showed how they played, this chart proves why they won. You can see Leicester’s blue line rising at a near-perfect, relentless 45-degree angle, illustrating a steady accumulation of points without the “flat spots” (losses and draws) that plague their rivals. In contrast, the lines for Arsenal (Red) and Tottenham (White) are jagged, showing periods of promise followed by stalls and collapses. The graph effectively debunks the myth that Leicester’s win was a fluke; luck looks like a spike, but a straight diagonal line over 38 games represents superior, long-term consistency.

The 5000-1 Odds

If you bet £10 on Leicester at the start of the season, you would have won £50,000. It remains the biggest sporting upset in history.
